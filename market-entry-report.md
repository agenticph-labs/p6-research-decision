## 3. Market Sizing

```
Total Population (2025): 115.9M
  ↓ Urban population (49.5%): 57.4M
    ↓ Coffee drinkers (35% of urban): 20.1M
      ↓ Specialty-addressable (18%): 3.6M consumers
```

## 4. Competitor Analysis

### 4.1 Market Structure

The Philippine coffee market spans four competitive tiers:

| Tier | Price Range | Key Players | Characteristics |
|------|------------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Budget** | PHP 95-130 | Dunkin', Pickup Coffee | High volume, low margin, convenience-oriented |
| **Mass-Market** | PHP 140-160 | Tim Hortons, Figaro, Bo's Coffee | Broad appeal, value positioning, 75+ stores each |
| **Mass-Premium** | PHP 185-195 | Starbucks, The Coffee Bean | Brand-driven, 380+ and 45 stores respectively |
| **Specialty** | PHP 195-225 | Yardstick, Commune, H Proper Coffee | Quality-led (8.5-9.0/10), small footprint, 5-12 stores |

### 4.2 Strategic Gap Analysis

The strategic group map reveals a **positioning gap** at the intersection of:
- **Price**: PHP 170-190 (mid-premium, below Starbucks but above mass-market)
- **Quality**: 8.0+ out of 10 (approaching specialty tier)

No current player occupies this slot with significant scale. Starbucks operates at higher price with lower quality scores (mass-produced). Local specialty roasters offer high quality but with very limited store counts and inconsistent experience.

### 4.3 Porter's Five Forces

| Force | Score (1-10) | Assessment |
|-------|-------------|------------|
| **Threat of New Entrants** | 7 | Low barriers at the cafe level, but specialty creates moats (roasting expertise, supply chain, brand) |
| **Supplier Power** | 6 | 70%+ imported beans → exposed to global arabica prices and FX; limited local supply |
| **Buyer Power** | 5 | Medium switching costs; loyalty driven by habit, experience, and third-place attachment |
| **Threat of Substitutes** | 5 | Tea, milk tea, energy drinks are alternatives but specialty coffee occupies a differentiated ritual/social slot |
| **Competitive Rivalry** | 8 | HIGH — Starbucks dominates with 380 stores; Tim Hortons growing fast; local chains entrenched; new specialty entrants emerging |

**Key finding:** High rivalry (8/10) is the principal headwind. Every other force is manageable with the right strategy.

## 7. Risk Assessment

### 7.1 Risk Register

| Risk | Score | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|-------|------------|--------|------------|
| Supply chain — bean price volatility | 42 | 7 | 6 | Multi-year contracts, futures hedging, 3-month buffer stock |
| Brand reputation — quality inconsistency | 42 | 6 | 7 | Centralized roasting, strict SOPs, mystery shoppers, continuous training |
| Real estate — CBD competition | 35 | 7 | 5 | 10-year leases; explore kiosk/pop-up formats to secure secondary locations |
| Regulatory — permit delays | 32 | 4 | 8 | Regulatory consultant; parallel applications; built-in 6-month lead time |
| Competitive — price war | 32 | 8 | 4 | Differentiate on quality; loyalty program; avoid head-to-head price competition |
| Macro — peso depreciation | 30 | 6 | 5 | FX hedging; local supplier development; pass-through pricing model |
| Talent — barista shortage | 30 | 5 | 6 | In-house training academy, above-market pay, career progression |
| Climate — El Niño impact | 20 | 5 | 4 | Diversify sourcing; maintain buffer stock |
| Technology — disruption | 15 | 3 | 5 | API-first architecture; multi-platform integration |

### 7.2 Key Mitigations for High-Severity Risks

1. **Supply chain**: Lock in multi-year contracts with Colombian and Vietnamese suppliers within first 90 days. Build 3-month roasted bean inventory at the central roastery.
2. **Quality consistency**: The central roastery model (one facility serving all stores) is the single most important operational control. Every store follows the same SOP, dial-in, and recipe.
3. **CBD real estate**: Engage a dedicated PH retail real estate broker at Phase 0. Target buildings with co-working tenants (WeWork, Regus) for built-in traffic.

## 9. Conclusion

The Philippine specialty coffee market is a **high-potential, high-execution-risk opportunity**. The demographic fundamentals are compelling — a young, urbanizing population with rising disposable income and a deepening coffee culture. The strategic gap in the mid-premium specialty tier offers a genuine entry point.

However, the market is competitive, real estate is constrained, and specialty coffee talent is scarce. Success depends not on the strategic concept but on **operational execution**: central roasting, talent development, location selection, and capital discipline.

**Proceed with Phase 0. Conditional GO — not a blank check.**
